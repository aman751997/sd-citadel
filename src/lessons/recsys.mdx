## The framing that makes this an SD question

"Design TikTok's feed / Netflix recommendations" is not an ML modeling interview — it's a **systems** question about serving personalized rankings over millions of items in ~200 ms. The models are boxes with latency and freshness properties. Get the funnel right and you pass; derive loss functions and you've answered the wrong question.

## The two-stage funnel (the answer's skeleton)

You cannot score 100M items per request. Every real system funnels:

<Mermaid code={`flowchart LR
    CORPUS["100M items"] --> CG["Stage 1 — candidate generation\ncheap, recall-oriented\n~few ms → 1,000 candidates"]
    CG --> RK["Stage 2 — ranking\nexpensive model, precision\n~50-100ms → ordered top 50"]

Say the shape first, then fill stages. **Stage 1 optimizes recall cheaply** ("don't miss anything plausibly good"), **Stage 2 spends compute on precision** over a small set. The business layer after both is where product rules live (no 10 videos from the same creator, boost fresh content, policy filters) — mentioning it separates people who've shipped feeds from people who've read papers.

## Two-tower — how candidate generation works

The bank names it, so know it properly: two neural nets — a **user tower** (history, demographics → user vector) and an **item tower** (content, stats → item vector) — trained so that engagement ≈ dot-product similarity. The serving trick that makes it a systems answer:

- **Item vectors precompute offline** (items change slowly) → loaded into an **ANN index** — literally the same HNSW machinery as the RAG lesson.
- **User vector computes at request time** (one cheap forward pass — it must reflect what you did 5 minutes ago).
- Candidate generation = ANN lookup: "1,000 nearest items to this user vector, in milliseconds."

One architecture sentence connects three lessons: *"candidate gen is an ANN search over precomputed item embeddings — the vector-search infrastructure from RAG, reused."* Multiple sources feed candidates in practice (two-tower + followed-creators + trending from the time-series pattern) — union them, let the ranker sort it out.

## The feature store — the infra piece interviewers probe

The ranker wants features: user's 7-day category clicks, item's 1-hour engagement rate, user×item history. The **feature store** serves them with two paths (and one trap):

- **Batch path**: heavy aggregates computed offline (Spark, daily) → pushed to a low-latency KV store (Redis/Dynamo) for serving.
- **Streaming path**: real-time counters (item's last-hour CTR) via the time-series pattern's aggregators.
- **The trap — training/serving skew**: if training computes "7-day clicks" one way and serving another, the model silently underperforms. The feature store's whole reason to exist is *one definition, both consumers*. Name the skew; it's the senior detail.

Freshness ladder to recite: ranking model retrains daily-ish · item embeddings hours · streaming features seconds-minutes · user vector per-request. Matching each component to its natural freshness *is* the design.

## Closing the loop

Impressions and engagements flow back (event stream → the ad-aggregator pipeline) into training data and streaming features. Two one-sentence flags that earn credit: **feedback loops** (recommend clicks → clicks train recommendations → filter bubble; countered with exploration ε and diversity rules) and **cold start** (new user: onboarding interests + popularity prior; new item: content-based embedding until engagement accrues).
