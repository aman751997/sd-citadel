## The point of estimation (it is not math)

Interviewers don't care whether you land on 11,600 QPS or 12,000 QPS. They care whether your numbers **end in design decisions**. Every estimate must terminate in a sentence like:

> "~12K read QPS → a single Postgres won't enjoy that → cache tier in front."

No conclusion, no credit. That's the whole game.

## The 4 axes — compute in this order

| Axis | Question it answers | Design decision it feeds |
|------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| **QPS** | How many requests hit us per second? | # of app servers, cache or not, LB needed |
| **Storage** | How big does the dataset get? | One DB vs shards, retention policy |
| **Bandwidth** | Bytes/sec in and out? | CDN or not — usually: *decide what to ignore* |
| **Memory** | Does the working set fit in RAM? | 1 cache node vs cluster |

## Axis 1 — QPS: the ÷10⁵ rule

A day is 86,400 seconds. **Round it to 10⁵.** The error (13%) is smaller than the error in your DAU guess anyway.

```
requests/day ÷ 100,000 = average QPS
```

- 1M requests/day → **10 QPS** (tiny — one server, done)
- 100M requests/day → **1,000 QPS** (real service — LB + several app servers)
- 10B requests/day → **100,000 QPS** (big tech — cache tier mandatory, regional deployment conversation)

Then **peak**: multiply average by 2–3× and *say the assumption out loud* — "assuming peak is ~3× average, that's 3K QPS peak." Interviewers reward stated assumptions and punish silent ones.

Read/write split next: "100:1 read-heavy" instantly justifies caching and read replicas. Always name the ratio.

## Axis 2 — Storage: carry units up the ladder

The method: `records/day × bytes/record`, then **climb the unit ladder one rung at a time**, saying each rung.

Byte-size anchors worth memorizing:

| Thing | Size |
|-------|------|
| UUID / long ID | 16 B / 8 B |
| Short text row (URL mapping, like) | ~100 B–500 B |
| Tweet-ish record with metadata | ~1 KB |
| Photo (compressed) | ~200 KB–1 MB |
| Minute of video (1080p) | ~50 MB |

Worked example — URL shortener, 1M new URLs/day, ~500 B/row:

```
1M × 500 B = 500 MB/day
× 365      ≈ 180 GB/year
× 5 years  ≈ 1 TB
```

Conclusion sentence: "1 TB over five years — a single Postgres instance holds this comfortably; sharding is not a day-one problem." **That sentence is the deliverable, not the 1 TB.**

## Axis 3 — Bandwidth: decide what to ignore

Bandwidth = QPS × payload size. For most text-payload systems the answer is "kilobytes per request at thousands of QPS → tens of MB/s → a non-issue, moving on." Saying *"bandwidth is a non-issue here, I'll skip it"* is a **correct and strong answer** — it shows judgment.

It matters when payloads are media: video streaming, photo feeds, file upload. Then: CDN, presigned upload URLs, and the estimate decides CDN egress vs origin.

## Axis 4 — Memory: working set → 1 node or cluster?

The question is never "how big is the data" — it's **"how big is the HOT data?"** The 80/20 heuristic: ~20% of data serves ~80% of traffic; often even steeper.

Rate limiter example — 50K API keys, one counter each:

```
50K keys × ~100 B (key + counter + timestamp) = 5 MB
```

Conclusion: "5 MB — fits in one Redis node's RAM a thousand times over; a cluster would be for availability, not capacity." Distinguishing **capacity-driven** from **availability-driven** clustering is a senior-level sentence — use it.

## The two killers (caught live in your own reps)

**Killer 1 — the unit slip.** GB↔TB confusion produces silent 1000× errors that poison every downstream conclusion. Antidote: climb the ladder **one rung at a time, out loud** — KB → MB → GB → TB. Never jump two rungs in your head.

**Killer 2 — carrying stale numbers.** Reusing the previous problem's (or previous axis's) numbers because they're still on the whiteboard. Antidote: each axis starts by **re-stating its inputs from scratch**: "1M new URLs a day at 500 bytes each…"

## The full sweep, end to end

For any problem, this is the 5-minute script:

1. "Let me get the numbers before designing."
2. QPS: daily volume ÷ 10⁵, ×3 peak, read/write ratio → *conclusion*
3. Storage: rows/day × bytes, ladder to 5 years → *conclusion*
4. Bandwidth: payload × QPS → usually *"non-issue, skipping"*
5. Memory: hot set size → *1 node vs cluster, capacity vs availability*
6. Bridge: "The numbers say [X], so here's the shape I'd start with."

Then once more on **Twitter timeline reads**. Numbers are allowed to be rough; missing conclusion sentences are not.
